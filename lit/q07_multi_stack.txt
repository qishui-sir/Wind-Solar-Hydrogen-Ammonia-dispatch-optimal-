TITLE   : Green hydrogen integration in refineries: optimizing multi-stack electrolyzer and steam-methane reformer operation under renewable intermittency and market exposure
AUTHORS : Riccardo Travaglini; Dimitrios Xevgenos; Kenneth Bruninx
JOURNAL : 
YEAR    : 2026  | CITED: 0 | TYPE: posted-content
DOI     : https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7135493
ABSTRACT: While Steam Methane Reforming (SMR) remains the dominant production technology in the refining sector, its high carbon intensity necessitates transitional pathways that integrate renewable hydrogen production without compromising supply reliability. However, the decarbonization of hydrogen supply is constrained by process requirements that rarely align with the intermittency of renewable energy. This work presents a techno-economic optimization framework for a hybrid hydrogen production system coupling an existing SMR unit with a grid-connected electrolyzer operating under a wind Power Purchase Agreement and spot market exposure.The model minimizes total operational hydrogen supply costs under strict demand constraints, allowing for a component-level electrolyzer representation, explicitly modeling multi-stack modularity, variable efficiency, degradation dynamics, and operational state transitions. This enables joint optimization of internal stack scheduling and system-level dispatch, capturing asymmetric module behavior neglected in most of conventional models.Applied to a refinery case study, the hybrid configuration reduces operational costs by up to 50% compared with the SMR-only baseline. Optimal stack scheduling converges to an overall uniform load distribution at the end of the optimization span that balances module utilization and minimizes asymmetric degradation. Sensitivity analysis identifies hydrogen remuneration schemes as the dominant economic driver, inducing variations in the levelized cost of hydrogen of up to 57%, while technical parameters such as stack capacity and storage size exert comparatively limited influence. These findings provide a transferable framework for economically viable, brownfield industrial hydrogen decarbonization.
