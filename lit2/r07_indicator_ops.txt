TITLE   : Stochastic Flexibility Quantification for Urban Integrated Energy Systems: Definition, Modeling, and Computation
AUTHORS : Linjuehao Mei; Lei Yan; Junzhe Huang; keyi chen
JOURNAL : 
YEAR    : 2026  | CITED: 0 | TYPE: posted-content
DOI     : https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7199502
ABSTRACT: Urban integrated energy systems (UIESs) couple electricity, heating, and natural-gas systems; consequently, quantifying their ability to accommodate stochastic net power demand (NPD) forecast errors—i.e., their stochastic flexibility (SF)—differs from the corresponding task for conventional power systems. Accurate SF quantification faces two challenges. First, different conversion efficiencies of multi-energy coupling units may cause capacity-oriented methods to overexploit extremely low-probability scenarios and thereby deviate from practical needs. Second, the city-wide scale accentuates delay in gas and heating networks; neglecting the risk of state-limit violations beyond the scheduling period may bias SF estimates. To address these issues, this paper proposes an accommodable error range (AER) whose computation explicitly accounts for cross-period network safety constraints and develops probability—capacity SF metrics. The probability metric measures the likelihood that a prescribed error distribution can be accommodated, whereas, once this distribution is fully covered, the remaining physical regulation margin is reported by the capacity metric. A probability-prioritized two-stage robust optimization model is formulated to align the resulting quantification with actual forecast-error demand while retaining physical regulation margin. For cross-period constraints caused by network delay, an effective delay influence horizon is identified from the decay of response upper bounds, thereby incorporating post-period state limits into the metric model. To reduce the computational burden of the resulting large-scale, long-horizon model, a redundant-boundary reduction algorithm is developed from local dependence relations among nodal states. Case studies verify the effectiveness and computational efficiency of the proposed method.

TITLE   : Automation of greenhouse gas emissions quantification in natural gas production for operational flexibility
AUTHORS : Hillmert Solano; Disha Gadre; Pallav Sarma
JOURNAL : Australian Energy Producers Journal
YEAR    : 2026  | CITED: 0 | TYPE: journal-article
DOI     : https://doi.org/10.1071/ep25079
ABSTRACT: This paper covers a case study demonstrating how automating greenhouse gas (GHG) calculations can improve accuracy, transparency, and efficiency across natural gas operations. This US case study from the Appalachian Basin shows how operational activity data and measurement inputs can be mapped directly into emissions estimation models, producing comprehensive inventories for carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and nitrous oxide (N2O). The automated methodology reduces human error common in spreadsheet-based reporting, integrates source-level emissions factors, and enables near real-time identification of emission-intensive processes. Beyond inventory generation, the framework supports emissions forecasting, allowing operators to model the impacts of operational changes, reduction technologies, and production planning on future carbon and methane intensities. The paper also discusses the potential of how requirements from Australian reporting frameworks, including the National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting Scheme, can be incorporated into automated workflows. Overall, the approach positions automated GHG quantification as both a compliance and operational intelligence capability supporting methane mitigation across upstream and midstream assets.
