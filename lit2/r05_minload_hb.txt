JOURNAL : Tekhnichna Elektrodynamika
YEAR    : 2026  | CITED: 0 | TYPE: journal-article
DOI     : https://doi.org/10.15407/techned2026.03.003
ABSTRACT: The paper considers features of consumer protection against microsecond high-voltage surges from the power grid under consumer’s partial load operation. A typical protection circuit containing a cascade-connected voltage limiter and a two-stage LC low-pass filter (LPF) is analyzed. Theoretical calculations and simulation studies of the response of the L-shaped LPF to an input voltage surge under the partial load operation of the electrical consumer were carried out, which showed an increase in the response amplitude. To reduce the response of the LPF, it is proposed to introduce resistors in series with each capacitor of the LPF. The results of simulation and experimental verification confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed addition – the maximum voltage surge at the consumer’s AC input decreased by up to 2 times. TITLE   : Assessment of the operation of a pre-chamber marine engine fuelled by ammonia-hydrogen mixtures at full and partial load
AUTHORS : G. D’Antuono; D. Lanni; E. Galloni; G. Fontana
JOURNAL : Journal of Physics: Conference Series
YEAR    : 2025  | CITED: 0 | TYPE: journal-article
DOI     : https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/3143/1/012098
ABSTRACT: Abstract Carbon-free fuels can play a crucial role in the decarbonization of the maritime sector. This research examines the operation of a pre-chamber marine engine fuelled with ammonia-hydrogen mixtures. The study is carried out by means of a previously validated three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics model. Both full and partial load conditions have been assessed. At full load with 15% hydrogen in the fuel mixture, a spark angle of 716° is necessary to avoid knocking. The engine can achieve an IMEP H of 29 bar, approximately 43% efficiency, and a gross power output of 1.1 MW per cylinder. In these conditions, unburned ammonia and NO 2 emissions are relatively low, N 2 O emissions are zero, while NO remains the main pollutant. At partial load conditions, a fuel mixture that contains 15% hydrogen by volume does not enable acceptable engine operation, whereas a 30% hydrogen content results in suitable operating conditions.

TITLE   : Beyond code-minimum U-values: building-scale energy, peak-load and cost trade-offs for wall insulation across Spanish climates
AUTHORS : Iván Felis Enguixa; Carmen Álvarez García
JOURNAL : 
YEAR    : 2026  | CITED: 0 | TYPE: posted-content
DOI     : https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7284914
ABSTRACT: Minimum U-value requirements secure a baseline level of envelope performance, but they do not identify when more insulation remains useful after compliance. This study develops a building-scale, code-constrained decision framework that separates regulatory, energy, peak-load and economic thicknesses. A 48 m^2 single-zone dwelling was simulated hourly under official Spanish residential schedules in five climates. Seven wall configurations from traditional, lightweight and prefabricated families were evaluated from their exact local CTE minimum to 200 mm of expanded polystyrene, producing 576 designs. A physics-based finite-volume state-space model represented layer order and thermal storage; its 20 mm production mesh differed from a 5 mm reference by at most 0.068% in annual electricity and 0.42% in peak loads. Four above-code criteria were compared: a 1% marginal energy knee, 95% energy-savings saturation, a normalized energy–peak–thickness compromise, and a 30-year material-only life-cycle-cost minimum. The marginal knee added 0"-" 58 mm and saved 0–10.6% relative to code-minimum designs. In contrast, 95% saturation frequently required 170"-" 190 mm, while cooling peaks changed by only -3.3% to +3.0% and free-running summer overheating increased by up to 27.6%. Under the deliberately favourable material-only cost boundary, economic additions were only 0"-" 18 mm; the maximum supportable marginal installed cost was 2.04 € m^(-2) cm^(-1). Across 1,024 economic scenarios, the code minimum retained the lowest cost in 75.3–100% of representative cases. The results reject a universal optimal thickness: code compliance must be followed by climate-, operation- and objective-specific appraisal, with economic conclusions reported as transparent break-even thresholds rather than fixed market prices.
